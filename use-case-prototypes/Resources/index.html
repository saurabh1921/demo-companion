One truck, four IDs, five systems. One governed record.

Why equipment data fragments

Every system was bought for a different job

Maintenance, dispatch, the OEMs, IT service management and the roster each identify a machine their own way, and each is right about something.

Systems of record grew up apart

SAP owns the asset register, criticality and service plan; dispatch owns live status and location; telematics owns the hours meter; ServiceNow owns the onboard technology; the roster owns the maintenance crew.

Identity is the weak link

HT214, HT 214 and HT-214. Serials with spaces in SAP and dashes in dispatch. A CMDB CI with a free-text asset tag and no SAP number. A swapped telematics gateway reporting a serial nobody knows.

Safety and cost raise the stakes

An assistant reading raw systems repeats their contradictions. Governed data products give one answer, keep worker health private, refuse to help bypass safety controls and route spend over AUD 20,000 to the superintendent.

"Doesn't the ServiceNow CMDB already do this?"

The CMDB stays the system of record for onboard technology (onboard computer, telematics gateway, collision avoidance, firmware) and for the CI relationships incident and change management depend on. Data Hub takes that value from ServiceNow and never overrules it.

Data Hub is the referee that keeps every system agreeing on which machine it is: it matches SAP, dispatch, telematics, ServiceNow and the roster across their four IDs, decides which system wins each field, and syncs both ways, so the CMDB is a source and a consumer: its CIs end up carrying the SAP number and the clean fleet ID.
