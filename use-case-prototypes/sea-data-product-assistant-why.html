South-East Asia · the monthly reporting cycle

Which number goes to the board?

Four subsidiaries — Singapore, Indonesia, Vietnam and Thailand. Two ERP systems: SAP, from the original regional build, and Oracle EBS, inherited with acquisitions. Neither is going away. Neither is wrong.

The situation

Every month head office asks the region how it did. That answer goes into the board pack, and it decides where next quarter’s capital goes and which markets get headcount.

Producing it means pulling from both ERPs, because both hold real trading activity. There is nothing unusual about that — almost every regional business that grew by acquisition runs more than one ledger. The problem is not that the systems exist. It is that nobody has decided which of them to believe, so the answer depends on who you ask.

So this month, we asked

“How did South-East Asia do last month?”

Two analysts. Both competent, both pulling from the systems of record, both working the same month. Here is what came back.

Same month. Same four subsidiaries. Same two source systems. The difference is not arithmetic — it is that the two systems do not agree on what they are describing.

Why this one is dangerous

A gap you can see is an inconvenience. This one you cannot: no error, no warning, no blank cell, nothing in a log. The report runs, the deck gets built, and the number is wrong by a quarter of the region. The only way anyone finds out is if two people happen to compare notes — which is exactly how this was found.

The same root cause, a different symptom

Everything above surfaced as a wrong number. But the underlying problem is not really about money — it is that two systems each hold a version of the truth and nothing decides between them. So it shows up wherever you look.

Ask a far simpler question — who is accountable for this entity? — and the same two systems hand you two different people. For all four subsidiaries.

Four entities, eight names — again nobody is wrong

The finance system holds the name that was correct when the entity was set up. Operations holds the person running it today. Both look authoritative. Neither is labelled stale.

The cost here is not money, it is time and control: escalations go to whoever the asker happened to look up, approvals stall behind someone who moved on, and nobody finds out they used the wrong name until the deadline has passed.

Why this is the clearest case for a golden record

This is an attribute, not a measure. There is one right answer and the business can name who decides it — so it can be resolved once, centrally, and read by everyone. Revenue cannot: if two ledgers disagree on revenue you have a reconciliation to do, not a winner to pick. Master data is for the facts that describe an entity, never the facts that measure it.

What we actually changed

Notice what neither problem needed: a new warehouse, a new dashboard, or a migration off one of the ERPs. Both had the same missing thing — nobody had decided which system is authoritative for which fact.

So that is what changed. Not a new report: a decision about who owns which fact, recorded once in a place the systems enforce rather than in the head of whoever built the spreadsheet. Every one of these is a business call, not a technical one.

One company, one ID Identity

Every subsidiary is matched on its legal entity ID, not its name. Eight records collapse to four companies — automatically, every time, no manual tidy-up.

Finance decides Currency

SAP is authoritative for what currency an entity reports in. Oracle's value is kept but does not win. Indonesia is IDR because Finance says so.

Operations decides Trading status

Oracle is authoritative for whether an entity is trading. Thailand reads Dormant everywhere downstream, including in the answer an executive is given.

Operations decides Accountability

Oracle is authoritative for who runs the entity, because the finance system’s copy goes stale when people move. Ask who owns Vietnam and you get one name.

Finance decides Tax & contact

SAP is authoritative for the tax registration and the finance mailbox. The same record can have different systems winning different fields — that is the point.
